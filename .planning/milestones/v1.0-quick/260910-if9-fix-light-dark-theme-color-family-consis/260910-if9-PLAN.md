<objective>
Close the light/dark colour-family inconsistency the developer reported on the game detail page:
(1) the masthead info pills (players / tiempo / dificultad) read as a pale near-grey lavender in
light but a saturated purple in dark, and (2) dark's secondary/metadata labels read as
visually disabled rather than as a legible secondary tier.

Purpose: quick task 260910-hdc moved dark's *ink* tokens onto the brand hue (H313.1) but left
dark's *base ladder* — the very tokens the pills draw their fill and border from — at H298–301.
That is a measurable 14.6-degree intra-theme split that light does not have (light's pill fill and
ink sit ~1–3 degrees apart). This task measures that split across every pill tone, gets the
developer's decision on how far to correct it, and applies the decision behind a tripwire.

Output: a reproducible OKLCh audit script + findings table, a developer-chosen fix applied in
`assets/css/app.css` through the existing `--pk-*` / daisyUI token system, and regression tests
extending the existing OKLCh tripwire block.
</objective>

Two independent findings fall out of this:

**F1 — dark has an intra-theme hue split that light does not.** `.pk-pill-neutral` draws its fill
from `--color-base-200` and its ink from `--color-neutral`. In light those are 0.9 degrees apart
(H308.2 vs H307.3). In dark they are **14.6 degrees apart** (H298.5 vs H313.1). `.pk-pill-outline`
has the same shape via `--color-base-300` (H298.1 vs H313.1 = **15.0 degrees**). Root cause:
260910-hdc rotated dark's ink family onto the brand hue and left dark's base ladder where sketch
054 put it. Every pill whose surface comes from the base ladder inherited the split.

**F2 — the cross-theme chroma of the pill surface is 6x apart.** Light's pill fill is C0.020 (a
near-achromatic wash); dark's is C0.119. Same token name, same role, two very different
saturations — this is what makes the two renderings read as different families rather than
light/dark variants of one.

**F3 — dark's label hierarchy gap is already TIGHTER than light's, not looser.** Secondary label
ink vs primary body ink: light is L49.9 → L23.0 (Δ26.9); dark is L75.2 → L95.3 (Δ20.1). Dark's
muted label already sits proportionally closer to primary text than light's does, and measures
6.85:1 on `--color-base-100`. So the reported "reads as disabled" is not a contrast failure and is
not a lightness gap that is wider than light's — meaning lifting dark's label toward white
*shrinks* an already-tighter gap. This is the fact that makes the label question a real decision
rather than an obvious fix.
</measured_baseline>
