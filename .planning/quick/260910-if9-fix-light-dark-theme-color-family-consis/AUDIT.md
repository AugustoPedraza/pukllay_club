## Findings

**F1 — dark has an intra-theme hue split that light does not.** `.pk-pill-neutral` draws its
fill from `--color-base-200` and its ink from `--color-neutral`. In light those sit 0.9 degrees
apart (fill H308.2 vs ink H307.3 — actually the tone's *max* spread across fill/border/ink is
2.8 degrees, driven by border at H310.1). In dark, fill sits at H298.5 and ink at H313.1 — a
14.6-degree fill-vs-ink split — and the tone's own *border* (`--color-base-300`, H298.1) is even
further from ink, producing the reported 15.0-degree max intra-tone spread. `.pk-pill-outline`
and the bare `.pk-chip` share the exact same mechanism (both read border from `--color-base-300`
and ink from `--color-neutral`): 15.0 degrees in dark, 2.8 in light. Root cause confirmed exactly
as the plan's `<measured_baseline>` states: quick task 260910-hdc rotated dark's *ink* family
(`--color-neutral`, `--pk-ink-brand`) onto the brand hue (H313.1) but left dark's *base ladder*
(`--color-base-100/200/300`, H298.1–300.7) where sketch 054 originally put it. Every pill/chip
whose surface comes from the base ladder inherited the split; light never had this problem because
light's whole palette — base ladder and ink alike — already sits in a tight 300.1–310.1 degree
band.

**F2 — the cross-theme chroma of the pill surface is ~6x apart.** `.pk-pill-neutral`'s fill is
C0.020 in light (a near-achromatic wash) and C0.119 in dark (measured ratio 5.94x, plan's
`<measured_baseline>` rounded this to "6x"). Same token role (`base-200` as pill fill), two very
different saturations — this is the second half of why the two themes' pills read as different
colour families rather than light/dark variants of one: even where the hue split (F1) is small,
the chroma jump alone changes how "coloured" the pill reads.

**F3 — dark's label hierarchy gap is already tighter than light's, not looser.** Freshly measured:
light's secondary-label ink (`--color-neutral`, L49.9) to primary body ink
(`--color-base-content`, L23.0) is ΔL 26.9. Dark's secondary-label ink (`--color-neutral`, L75.2)
to primary body ink (`--color-base-content`, L95.3) is ΔL 20.1. Dark's gap is *narrower*, not
wider, and dark's label ink measures 6.85:1 against `--color-base-100` (#2F154E) — well clear of
the 4.5:1 WCAG floor. So the reported "reads as disabled" is neither a contrast failure nor a
lightness-gap failure relative to light; it is consistent with the perception being driven by F1/F2
(the chip surface, not the label ink itself) or by chroma alone (dark's label ink chroma is
C0.100, only 8% of the way from grey to dark's own most-saturated fill C0.21, even though its
lightness gap is proportionally tight). This is exactly why the label question in Task 2 is a real
decision and not an obvious fix — lifting dark's label toward white *shrinks* an already-tighter
gap.

**Caveat — two summary FAIL rows are not part of this task's scope and should not be read as
additional bugs.** `.pk-pill-selected` shows spreads of 149.8/136.8 degrees, and
`.pk-pill-accent`/`.pk-chip.is-active` show a 9.9-degree FAIL in light. Both are measurement
artefacts / out-of-scope findings, not part of Task 2's chip-family or label-ink decision:

- `.pk-pill-selected`'s ink role is `--color-primary-content`, which is pure white (`#FFFFFF`,
  C0.000) in both themes. OKLCh hue is mathematically undefined at zero chroma (the script's
  `atan2` still returns a number — H89.9 — but it carries no perceptual meaning against a
  saturated fill). White-ink-on-solid-fill is a deliberate, correct pattern here, not a
  hue-family defect; the "FAIL" is the threshold algorithm being applied somewhere it does not
  apply, not a real problem to fix.
- `.pk-pill-accent` (light) and `.pk-chip.is-active` (light) both fail at 9.9 degrees — driven by
  `--color-accent-content` (H300.1) sitting 9.9 degrees from `--color-accent`/`--color-primary`
  fill (H309.9). This is a genuine, small intra-theme spread, but neither tone is in Task 2's
  option set (C1–C3 target `.pk-pill-neutral`/`.pk-pill-outline`/`.pk-chip`'s fill+border; W1–W3
  target label ink, not accent-content). Per the plan's scope-narrowing rule, this is flagged here
  for visibility and left alone unless the developer asks for it in a follow-up — it is not part
  of this task's fix.

### Chip family

**C1 — retone the pill surface only.** Dark-scope `.pk-pill-neutral`/`.pk-pill-outline`/`.pk-chip`'s
fill and border to `color-mix(in oklab, var(--pk-ink-brand) X%, var(--color-base-100) (100-X)%)`,
landing the pill surface on the ink's own hue. Blast radius: pills/chips only — no other dark
surface changes.

Candidate at X=15% (fill) / X=30% (border):
- fill: `#432663` — L33.8 C0.105 H303.1 (current fill: `#391B62` L30.7 C0.119 H298.5)
- border: `#583879` — L40.9 C0.109 H304.8 (current border: `#462278` L35.2 C0.139 H298.1)
- ink (`--color-neutral` #C59CDC, H313.1) on the new fill: **5.40:1** (clears the 4.5:1 text
  floor; current ink-on-fill is measurably higher still since the old fill is darker, but this
  candidate keeps comfortable margin)
- resulting intra-tone spread: fill-to-ink 10.0deg, border-to-ink 8.3deg — both improved from
  14.6/15.0 but not fully closed at this candidate percentage; higher percentages close the hue
  gap further but cost contrast margin (at X=35%/50%, ink-on-fill drops to 3.67:1/2.73:1, below
  the 4.5:1 floor) — there is a real percentage ceiling here, not a free lunch.

**C2 — rotate dark's base ladder onto the brand hue.** Recompute `--color-base-100/200/300`
holding L and C, moving H to 313.1 (matching `--color-primary`). Blast radius: every surface in
dark, not just pills.

- `--color-base-100`: `#2F154E` → `#361148` (L26.7 C0.101 H312.4, was H300.7)
- `--color-base-200`: `#391B62` → `#441659` (L30.8 C0.118 H313.1, was H298.5)
- `--color-base-300`: `#462278` → `#531B6D` (L35.2 C0.139 H312.7, was H298.1)
- Re-measured against sketch 054's four pinned assertions (dark theme block's own comment):
  - text (`#F3ECFA`) on bg (`#361148`): **13.67:1** (was 13.59:1 — holds)
  - muted (`#C59CDC`) on bg (`#361148`): **6.89:1** (was 6.85:1 — holds)
  - text (`#F3ECFA`) on surface (`#441659`): **12.09:1** (was 12.07:1 — holds)
  - primary-content (`#FFFFFF`) on primary (`#8C2BB6`): **6.70:1** (unchanged — primary itself is
    not touched by this option)
- All four pinned ratios survive the rotation with the same or slightly improved margin — the
  hue-only rotation does not reopen sketch 054's contrast decision. This is the option that
  actually closes F1 at its root (base ladder and ink both land on H312–313), at the cost of
  touching every dark surface, not just chips.

**C3 — equalise the cross-theme relationship instead of the hue.** Leave hues alone; derive BOTH
themes' pill fill from one shared `color-mix(in oklab, <theme's own ink>, <theme's own base-100>)`
recipe, so the pill is by construction the same relationship to its own ground in each theme.

Candidate at 8%:
- light fill: `#EEEBF4` — L94.5 C0.012 H301.3 (current: `#F3ECFA` L95.3 C0.020 H308.2); ink
  (`--color-neutral` #6B5B7B) on this fill: **5.23:1**
- dark fill: `#391E59` — L30.5 C0.103 H301.7 (current: `#391B62` L30.7 C0.119 H298.5); ink
  (`--color-neutral` #C59CDC) on this fill: **6.09:1**
- Both themes' fill is now derived by the identical formula (8% ink-into-base-100), so the
  cross-theme *relationship* is structurally symmetric. This does **not** close dark's own
  14.6/15.0-degree internal split (fill still sits apart from dark's ink hue by design — the
  mix pulls toward base-100's own hue, not toward the ink's), so C3 trades "provably symmetric
  across themes" for "still not a single hue family within dark itself."

### Label ink

**W1 — no change.** Per F3 above: dark's label-to-body lightness gap (ΔL 20.1) is already
narrower than light's (ΔL 26.9), and dark's label ink clears 6.85:1 against `--color-base-100`.
The "reads as disabled" report may be an artefact of the chip surface problem (F1/F2) rather than
the label ink itself — W1 is the honest option if the chosen chip fix (C1/C2/C3) alone resolves
the perception once viewed together on the real page.

**W2 — lift lightness, hold hue and chroma.** New theme-scoped `--pk-ink-label` token following
`--pk-ink-brand`'s exact shape (light: `var(--color-neutral)` read, so light cannot drift; dark: a
lifted value on `--color-neutral`'s own hue/chroma, H313.1 C0.100).

| Target L | Hex | Contrast on base-100 | Contrast on base-200 | Label-to-body ΔL (light's own: 26.9) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L80 | `#D4ABEC` | 8.10:1 | 7.19:1 | 15.3 |
| L83 | `#DEB4F6` | 8.94:1 | 7.94:1 | 12.3 |
| L85 | `#E5BBFD` | 9.61:1 | 8.53:1 | 10.3 |

All three candidates clear contrast comfortably and read visibly lighter than today's L75.2, at
the cost of narrowing the label-to-body gap further below dark's already-tighter 20.1 (dark's gap
would shrink to somewhere in the 10.3–15.3 range depending on target, versus light's 26.9) — the
exact tradeoff F3 flags.

**W3 — split the roles.** Lift only the uppercase section labels (`.pk-fact-col dt`,
`.pk-bgg-label`) to one of the W2 candidate values above (e.g. L83, `#DEB4F6`); leave the inline
sublabels (`.pk-bgg-lbl`, `.pk-bgg-foot`) on today's `--color-neutral` (`#C59CDC`, L75.2). This
introduces a fourth ink tier in dark — today's three deliberate tiers (per app.css's own
260910-hdc comment) are fill `--color-primary` (C0.21) > interactive ink `--pk-ink-brand` (C0.13)
> muted ink `--color-neutral` (C0.10); W3 would add a fifth distinct value between muted ink and
white, splitting what is currently one "muted/label" tier into two visually distinct lightnesses
depending on whether the label is a section heading or an inline stat label.
