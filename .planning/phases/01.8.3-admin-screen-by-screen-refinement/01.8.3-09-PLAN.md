<objective>
Close `G-01.8.3-2e`: make the search field grow with the viewport and put the `+`'s glyph on the
same right-hand axis as every row's chevron.

Purpose: the developer reported "the search and + should take same width that the rest (currently
there is padding at left and right)". The diagnosis found an AND of two conditions. The first is the
same page keel `G-01.8.3-2c` closed — with `<main>`'s `px-4` plus the row's own 16px, the field's
left border sits at 32, which is correct relative to the rows and needs no further change. The
second is the real defect and is entirely inside the search row: `juegos.css:64`'s
`#juegos-search-input { flex: 1; min-width: 0 }` is INERT, because the input's parent is
`<form id="juegos-search-form">` (`index.ex:641`), a plain `display: block` element which is itself
the flex ITEM of `.pk-admin-juegos-search-row`. The field never grows — it stands at its intrinsic
`size=20` width of 264px at 390, 375 AND 360 — so the `+` lands at a FIXED x=292 at every viewport
width. Its right edge therefore misses the rows' 32px chevron axis by 22 / 7 / -8 px at 390 / 375 /
360: dead space at 390, and at 360 the button already spills 8px past the row's own content box.
Aggravating it, `.pk-admin-action--a3`'s `margin-left: -12px` (`components.css:124`) is a
LEADING-icon pull ("pulled -12px so the glyph lands on the content edge") applied to a TRAILING
action, pushing the `+` further from the right keel instead of onto it.

Purpose, second half: `admin_shell.mjs` reads `search row left=16 right=16` and reports ALL CHECKS
PASSED. It measures the search-row CONTAINER's content box, which is genuinely correct relative to
the rows. The defect lives on boxes the probe never reads: the visible field's border box and the
`+`'s own rect. A guard for this gap must read the controls, not their container.

Output: one growth declaration on the element that is actually the flex item, one scoped trailing
pull on the `+`, and assertions that read the visible controls' rects at three widths — recorded
failing first.
