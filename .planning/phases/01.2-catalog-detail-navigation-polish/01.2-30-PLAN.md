<objective>
Give the lightbox's close button a fill it can actually be seen against, in dark theme only.

Round 7 (01.2-29) grew the lightbox stage to the full height of the screen and its own human-check
asked, in advance, whether the close button still read as a button once that large uniform dark
field arrived underneath it. UAT test 20 answered: "On the dark needs a little more constrant."

The diagnosis found the mechanism, and it is not a regression from round 7. `.pk-lightbox-close`
has never declared a fill of its own, and its markup carries no daisyUI colour modifier, so the
button inherits `.btn`'s unmodified default — whatever `--color-base-200` is in the active theme.
The surface behind it, though, is theme-INVARIANT: `--pk-shadow-color` is a single fixed dark
literal declared once in `:root`, and both the scrim and the opaque stage are built from it. A
theme-VARYING fill on a theme-INVARIANT backdrop is legible in one theme by construction and
illegible in the other. In light theme the default resolves near-white and measures over 10:1. In
dark theme it resolves #22103A against #140822 and measures 1.1:1 — the chip disappears and only
the near-white icon survives, which is exactly the "floating X, not a button" the user is looking
at. Round 7's height change did not cause this; it only made the dark backdrop consistently present
behind the button, and the plan that shipped it pre-flagged the risk and deferred the colour
deliberately: "that is a designed contrast value in a follow-up round, not a colour picked during
execution." This is that follow-up round.

So this plan adds one theme-scoped rule reading two existing tokens, and nothing else. It does not
touch the button's own rule, its position, its size, its stacking order, or its markup. It does not
touch light theme, because light theme is already at >10:1 and the same tokens applied there would
DROP it to ≈3.1:1 — the numbers are in the key links, and they are the whole reason this is scoped
rather than invariant.

Purpose: close G-01.2-20 — the last open item on the lightbox, severity cosmetic, one rule deep.
Output: a legible close button in dark theme, an assertion that COMPUTES the contrast from the
tokens rather than string-matching them, and the theme-asymmetry lesson recorded beside the
sketch-033 scrim lesson it mirrors.
</objective>
