<objective>
Collapse the detail page's two page-boundary gaps — header to masthead, and shelf to footer — to one
deliberate 24px value at each end, and stop the hidden sticky title bar from silently padding out the
first one.

The UAT reported both ends in the same breath: "there are too many space from where the details starts
and from the header" and "after the caoursel 'juegos recomendados' there are also too much whitespace
until the footer". Grounding both in the real code turned them into arithmetic rather than taste.

At the top, the sticky title-echo bar is unconditionally rendered and hidden only by fading its
opacity to zero. A sticky element still occupies its own box in normal document flow while invisible,
so roughly a bar's height of dead space sits between the header and the first thing a reader is meant
to see, on top of the page's own top padding.

At the bottom, four independently reasonable spacing declarations stack: the shelf's own trailing
margin, the page wrapper's bottom padding, the footer's top margin and the footer's inner top padding.
Sketch 035 counted three of them and measured 152px; the shelf's own margin is a fourth. Each is
defensible alone and the sum is not.

Three of those four live on shared, every-page surfaces. So the collapse is opt-in: one flag on the
shared layout, one scoped rule block, and every other page renders exactly as it does today.

Purpose: the page should start promptly under the header and end cleanly into the footer, with the
same rhythm at both ends.
Output: a title bar that costs no space while hidden, one opt-in boundary rule block, and a detail
page whose two ends measure the same.
</objective>
