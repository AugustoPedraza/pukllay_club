<objective>
Make the lightbox actually reach the shell's width — by giving its photo a real box instead of a
ceiling it was never tall enough to touch.

Plan 01.2-25 widened `.pk-lightbox-img`'s `max-width` from a standalone literal to the shell's own
content width. Two independent source-only verifier passes confirmed the change. Live UAT then found
the lightbox rendering exactly as before. Neither reading was wrong: `max-width` is an upper bound,
and every photo in this catalog is produced by the seed pipeline at 800px wide and near-square — under
the old cap, under the new cap, under any cap. The rule has no `width`, so the browser renders each
photo at its own 800 intrinsic pixels and both caps sit unused above it. The fix was real; the
constraint it moved was never the binding one.

So this plan stops adjusting a ceiling and gives the element a floor: an explicit width derived from
the same shell formula, an explicit height carrying forward the same vertical envelope, and — the part
that the user's actual complaint turns on — an opaque fill, so the space the photo does not fill is a
solid stage rather than more of the same see-through scrim. The photo is not upscaled and not cropped;
it letterboxes inside a box that finally has the page's own width.

Purpose: the reported symptom — "the background is adding noise", "the background is awful" — is a
small photo sitting in a large translucent field. Growing the opaque field is the lever that exists
without re-seeding every image in the catalog.
Output: a shell-width opaque stage, a corrected provenance record, and a regression guard that names
the trap so the next reader cannot fall into it.
</objective>
