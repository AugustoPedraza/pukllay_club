<objective>
Close G-01.2-8. The member's verbatim report: "There is a weird floating card at the top —
'Something went wrong! Attempting to reconnect' — This must to follow our visual identity."

Two causes act together, and only one of them is a bug.

**The behaviour is correct and is not going to change.** The catalog's `:more_error` inline retry
line fires when a live, connected round trip's database query fails. A devtools-offline condition
drops the websocket transport itself, before any `load-more` event can be delivered or replied to —
a categorically different failure mode. LiveView's own JS client detects the dropped socket and
toggles its connection-state body classes. That is not a defect and cannot be fixed by changing the
grid's retry code; the UAT truth that expected the inline line under a real offline condition was
describing something the architecture never routes there. Task 3 corrects that truth.

**The appearance is the bug.** The elements those body classes drive are verbatim, untouched
`mix phx.new` scaffolding: generic daisyUI toast and alert classes, not one `pk-` prefixed class
anywhere, no override of any LiveView connection-state selector in the stylesheet, and English
copy from the generator in an app that is otherwise entirely in Spanish. A top-right floating toast
in English is exactly the "weird floating card" the member described. This is the one surface in
the app that was never designed.

Purpose: the connection notice is, by definition, the thing a member sees when everything else has
stopped working. It is a poor moment for the product to stop looking like itself.
Output: a branded, Spanish, in-page connection notice; and a UI-SPEC that tells the truth about
what the grid's retry line can and cannot cover.
</objective>
